Charleston + Kiawah Island

Michelin stars, dolphin charters, and resort life done right.

The best blend of a true resort stay, easy family logistics, and now, real Michelin credibility. Charleston's Michelin selection shows 15 restaurants, including 3 one-stars and 3 Bib Gourmands. Kiawah gives you a genuine resort setup with villas, kids' pools, dolphin trips, and guided family kayaking.

Nights 1–3

The Charleston Place

The polished city-base move. Downtown Charleston, walkable to everything, two-bedroom suite options for the crew. This is where you feel fancy without trying.

Nights 4–7

Kiawah Island Resort Villa

Switch to full resort mode. A 3+ bedroom villa with resort access means pools, beach, and space to spread out. The Sanctuary is the prestige move, but a villa is smarter for seven people.

Day by Day

Day 1 Arrive & Settle Into Charleston +
Arrive in Charleston, check into The Charleston Place, keep the first night easy. Dinner at Sorelle — Michelin-listed, stylish, and a good "everyone feels like this trip is fancy" opener.
Day 2 The Big Food & City Day +
Your main food-and-city day. For lunch or an easier meal with kids: Leon's Oyster Shop, Lewis Barbecue, or Rodney Scott's BBQ. For the more adult-forward dinner: book Wild Common, Vern's, or Malagón Mercado y Taperia — all Michelin one-star picks. Explore the historic district, Rainbow Row, and the waterfront in between meals.
Day 3 Bulls Island Wildlife Adventure +
Animal and boating day from Charleston with the Bulls Island ferry. The naturalist-led trip includes dolphins, sea turtles, and birdlife on the ride over — a real wildlife note before switching into resort mode. Jax and Rey will love this one.
Day 4 Transfer to Kiawah — Pool Day +
Transfer to Kiawah and go full resort. Beach time, multiple pool complexes including the Night Heron pools with slides and spray features. This is the day Jax and Rey burn off their existence in chlorinated joy while the adults recover with something cold in hand.
Day 5 Private Dolphin Charter +
Book Kiawah's Dolphin Encounters as a private single-party boat. Their charter setup handles groups of 7 easily, and this is one of the cleanest low-hassle private wildlife outings available. The whole family on one boat, dolphins doing their thing.
Day 6 Family Kayaking with Naturalist +
Guided family kayaking with a Kiawah naturalist through Lowcountry marshes. Spot dolphins, birds, and maybe even bobcats. Built for families, not serious paddlers cosplaying as explorers. Easy pace, great scenery, memorable without being exhausting.
Day 7 Beach, Pool & Decompress +
Keep it light: beach, pools, or one of Kiawah's family activities like a sunset cruise or shelling expedition. You already got your wildlife hits — this is the decompression day instead of stuffing in another overplanned "memories" block.
Day 8 Depart (One Last Meal) +
Depart. If there's time, one last Michelin meal: FIG, Lowland, or The Restaurant at Zero George make for a strong final brunch or lunch depending on flight times.

Florida Keys + Everglades

Reef snorkeling, airboats, and alligators that don't care about your plans.

The stronger pick if you want the trip to lean harder into reef snorkeling, tropical water, and Everglades wildlife. Cheeca Lodge puts you on North America's only living coral barrier reef. The tradeoff: June puts you into Everglades wet season and the start of Atlantic hurricane season, and the most dependable Michelin anchors are in Miami/Surfside, not deep in the Keys.

Nights 1–2

Four Seasons at The Surf Club

Surfside/Miami. This is where the Michelin dining happens —The Surf Club Restaurant is a one-star pick. Beach, pool, and a refined launch point before heading south.

Nights 3–7

Cheeca Lodge & Spa, Islamorada

Oceanfront resort in the heart of the Keys. Snorkeling, kayaking, watersports on property, and group excursion coordination built in. Swap for Hawks Cay if you want more kid-focused activities.

Day by Day

Day 1 Arrive in Surfside +
Settle into the Four Seasons at The Surf Club. Dinner at The Surf Club Restaurant —Michelin one-star, and the cleanest way to satisfy the fancy dinner brief without turning the trip into a restaurant crawl with suitcases.
Day 2 Everglades Adventure Day +
Your big Everglades day while still on the Miami side. Two options: Miccosukee airboat + village/museum/alligator demo for max kid excitement, or the Shark Valley tram tour for a more naturalist, National Park vibe. That night: Michael's Genuine (Michelin Bib Gourmand, explicitly tagged as family-friendly) or Cote Miami if the adults want one more serious dinner.
Day 3 Drive to Islamorada +
Scenic drive down to the Keys. Check into Cheeca Lodge and use the day for pool, dock, beach, and a slow evening. Cheeca is oceanfront with watersports on property —let the tropical resort mode kick in gradually.
Day 4 Coral Reef Snorkeling +
Book Cheeca's snorkel tour to North America's only living coral barrier reef. This is the day that makes the Keys version feel meaningfully more "adventure" than Charleston. Tropical fish, reef formations, and genuinely unique marine life. Jax and Rey will talk about this one for years.
Day 5 Private Boat Excursion +
A private excursion or group watersports outing through Cheeca's on-property team. They handle group tours, private excursions, fishing trips, rentals, and lessons. This is the family boat day —customize it to what sounds fun.
Day 6 True Resort Day +
The whole point of paying for a nice Keys resort is not spending every hour in a car proving you are "maximizing" Florida. Beach, pool, paddle, fish, repeat. Zero agenda. Maximum relaxation.
Day 7 One More Water Day or Hawks Cay +
One more water activity at Cheeca, or if the kids want a big finale, hit Hawks Cay for their snorkel, sandbar, and dolphin-linked resort activities. Hawks Cay is more activity-heavy and more kid-tilted —a great backup or day trip option.
Day 8 Drive Back & Depart +
Drive back toward Miami and depart. Keep it simple —the trip has already delivered.

The Honest Comparison

Charleston + Kiawah

+ Best overall trip design for a group with kids + adults who like nice food. City + resort in one week.
+ 15 Michelin restaurants including 3 one-stars and 3 Bib Gourmands. Serious food city now.
+ Villa/home options on Kiawah mean real space for 7 people instead of pretending adjoining hotel rooms are a personality trait.
+ Private dolphin charters and family kayaking directly through Kiawah — easy logistics, built for families.
+ Lower weather risk in June compared to south Florida.
− Less tropical — Lowcountry marsh and dolphins, not coral reefs and tropical water. Different flavor of nature.
− No Everglades equivalent — the wildlife is beautiful but less dramatic than airboats and alligators.

Keys + Everglades

+ North America's only living coral barrier reef. Snorkeling here is a genuinely unique experience.
+ Everglades airboat day is unforgettable for kids. Alligators, airboats, the whole show.
+ More tropical and adventurous feel overall. This trip looks different from a normal beach vacation.
+ More water activities — snorkeling, boating, fishing, kayaking, all in warm tropical water.
− June = wet season + hurricane season. Everglades are already soggy. Atlantic hurricane season starts June 1. Real tradeoff.
− Michelin dining is a Miami add-on, not a Keys feature. The food story requires a split stay to work.
− More driving — Miami to Islamorada is ~90 min. The trip is a split-stay by design, not one clean resort week.

If you want the best balanced family trip, go Charleston + Kiawah. If you want the more memorable nature-and-water trip, go Florida Keys + Everglades and accept that the food piece is strongest when you tack on Miami.
