The Coffee Index

Seven downtown coffee shops, scored on what actually matters to us: who roasts the beans, whether the decaf is done right, a real pour-over and a proper double espresso, the bakery case, staying independent, and how far we have to go before caffeine. Hours verified June 2026.

01

Second State Coffee

35 / 39

The coffee-nerd’s pick

The only shop here that roasts its own beans (on a Loring S15), and it shows: a rotating wall of single origins across washed, natural, anaerobic and sugar-cane processes, both light and dark blends, and the best decaf in the field. Pour-over bar for you, Synesso espresso for Haley’s iced doubles. It wins on the two things you weighted heaviest.

The Rise Coffee Bar

29 / 39

Swiss Water decaf, garden patio

Inside The Restoration Hotel, pouring local Foresight Coffee. Their house decaf (“Nightcap”) is a genuine Swiss Water process, which is exactly your bar. Pour-over on the menu, breakfast sandwiches made in-house each morning (gone by 9am), and a garden courtyard that’s the nicest place to sit on this list. Open 7am to 5pm every day.

Harken Cafe

28 / 39

Best food, and 2 minutes away

The closest shop to the hotel and the strongest bakery in the field: everything baked on-site, a cheddar-chive scone people drive for, and an egg-soufflé sandwich on a house ricotta biscuit. Pours excellent Methodical beans (light roast available). The one open question is decaf, which we couldn’t confirm online; one phone call could move Harken to #2.

Kudu Coffee & Craft Beer

28 / 39

The OG, with a fountain courtyard

Charleston’s original (2005), single-location and proudly independent, pouring local Springbok on a La Marzocco FB80 with pour-over available. The shaded courtyard with a running fountain is the move, and it turns into coffee-and-a-beer by afternoon. Springbok’s decaf is Swiss Water but only made on request, and the food is the weak spot (brought-in pastries, no real breakfast sandwich).

Bad Bunnies Coffee

27 / 39

Purest indie, but it’s a hike

A tiny artist-owned shop whose owners have flatly refused to franchise. It pours Second State beans (so light roast and Swiss Water decaf, secondhand), runs a pour-over bar, and the matcha-honey latte has a cult following. The catch is distance: it’s up on Spring Street, a real ~29-minute walk or a quick 6-minute ride. House-made syrups, but pastries are brought in.

Best pastry case, weaker on the coffee specifics

A gorgeous European all-day cafe that pours Counter Culture and bakes its own pastries in-house (the banana bread is the standout). Lovely, but it ranks lower on your exact wants: no scone, no pour-over confirmed, decaf unverified, and it’s closed Mondays, which clips both 6/8 and 6/15.

The Daily

20 / 39

Trendiest, but misses both your must-haves

The viral bodega-cafe, pouring top-tier Onyx espresso with house sourdough and a pistachio-rose morning bun worth the hype. But for the two of you it lands last: no pour-over (batch drip only) and no decaf coffee program (decaf is tea-only on the menu), plus it’s a ~29-minute walk up King. Great spot, wrong fit.

How It’s Scored

Coffee & roast (×3) — roasts in-house or pours a top roaster; light and dark both available; single origins and processing variety.

Decaf (×3) — weighted as high as coffee, because we drink more decaf than caf. Swiss Water or specialty decaf, and reliably available.

Food & bakery (×2) — baked on-site, a good scone, a house-made breakfast sandwich.

Brew fit (×2) — a real pour-over and a proper double espresso for iced.

Independence (×2) — single indie shop over a local group over a chain.

Convenience (×1) — walk from the hotel, opens early, open the days we’re here.
